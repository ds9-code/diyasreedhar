Graph AI for Neurological Drug Repurposing

Explore Research

At the Harvard Kempner Institute for the Study of Natural and Artificial Intelligence, I contributed to PROTON (mims-harvard/PROTON), a 578-million-parameter heterogeneous graph transformer trained on NeuroKG - a brain-biology knowledge graph integrating 36 human datasets, 147,020 nodes across 16 entity types, 7.4 million edges across 47 relation types, and 3.76 million single-nucleus RNA-seq cells from the adult human brain. PROTON generates drug-repurposing hypotheses that have now been validated in molecular, organoid, and clinical systems. (arXiv:2512.13724.)

  • Heterogeneous graph transformer at scale. PROTON learns node embeddings for every entity type in NeuroKG - genes, proteins, cell types, brain regions, phenotypes, drugs - via node- and edge-type-aware multi-head attention with self-supervised link prediction. After Bayesian hyperparameter optimization, the model reaches AUROC = 0.9145 (accuracy = 82.23%) on a held-out test set.
  • Parkinson's disease - genome-wide α-synuclein experiments. PROTON reproduces the findings of six independent α-synuclein-pathology screens with normalized enrichment scores up to 2.30 (FDR < 1×10-4). Its pesticide predictions land in the top 6.75% of an ascertainment-bias sensitivity analysis on a 496-patient case-control cohort (NES = 2.13, FDR < 1×10-4).
  • Bipolar disorder - patient-derived cortical organoids. On a disease-centric split that withheld all drug information, PROTON nominated calcitriol; in patient-derived cortical organoids it reversed proteomic alterations across 73 dysregulated proteins, with effects distinct from those of existing BD treatments.
  • Alzheimer's disease - 610,524 patients. Of the model's top predicted drugs, retrospective analysis of 610,524 Mass General Brigham patient records associated five with reduced seven-year dementia risk (minimum hazard ratio 0.63, p < 1×10-7), surviving multiple-comparison correction.
  • Open release. Model weights are released through Hugging Face, NeuroKG through Harvard Dataverse (DOI 10.7910/DVN/ZDLS3K), and source under MIT license at mims-harvard/PROTON.
