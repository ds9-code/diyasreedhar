Wear- and Corrosion-Resistant Coatings for Spacesuit Materials

Explore Research

Through the NASA L'SPACE program, I led the materials-design track of a mission concept proposing extreme-environment coatings for crewed spacesuit and structural spaceship surfaces. The problem was realized when NASA's TP-2009-214786 report found that the three EVAs of the final Apollo mission damaged the outer fabric of the suits beyond what additional EVAs could tolerate, while Artemis-class operations demand far longer surface time on a regolith- and atomic-oxygen-rich envelope. As a brief summary of my research:

  • CALPHAD-guided alloy and ceramic design. I screened candidate coating chemistries with Thermo-Calc and Pandat for phase equilibria, transformation pathways, and sintering behavior across the LEO-to-lunar thermal window (~398 K day to ~96 K night, a ~300 °C peak-to-peak cycle). The pass shortlisted ~40 candidate systems from a several-hundred initial pool, biased toward phases stable under repeated thermal cycling and resistant to atomic-oxygen-driven oxidation. The reference point was NASA Glenn's GRX-810 (Co-Cr-Ni medium-entropy ODS alloy), which delivers ~2x strength at 1,100 °C and ~1,000x longer creep life than the prior nickel-superalloy baseline.
  • Nanoindentation and tribological characterization. I built a high-throughput nanoindentation protocol resolving hardness, elastic modulus, and wear rate across thin-film and bulk samples in a single sample-mounting pass, cutting characterization wall-clock by roughly 3x. Coupled to pin-on-disc tribometry, the protocol returned per-candidate Archard wear coefficients and adhesion-failure thresholds that fed back into the CALPHAD shortlist.
  • Microstructural analysis of degraded surfaces. I used SEM, TEM, EBSD, XRD, and optical microscopy to characterize as-deposited coatings and to map the evolution of grain-boundary chemistry and phase distribution after accelerated corrosion and abrasive-wear exposures. EBSD identified the grain-boundary populations preferentially nucleating corrosion-driven microcracks, informing a downstream texture-control intervention.
  • Mission-relevant performance outcomes. The resulting coating shortlist delivered measurable improvements over the Apollo-era beta-cloth and Teflon baselines on the metrics that dominate spacesuit and structural-spaceship durability: reduced wear rate under regolith-like abrasion, increased corrosion-resistance margin under simulated atomic-oxygen exposure, and stable hardness across thermal-cycle counts representative of an extended Artemis-class lunar surface mission.
