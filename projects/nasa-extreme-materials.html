Wear- and Corrosion-Resistant Coatings for Spacesuit Materials

Explore Research

Through the NASA L'SPACE (Lucy Student Pipeline Accelerator and Competency Enabler) program, I led the materials-design track of a mission concept proposing extreme-environment coatings for crewed spacesuit surfaces and structural spaceship interfaces - environments where lunar regolith abrasion, deep thermal cycling, atomic-oxygen flux, and prolonged corrosive exposure routinely degrade Apollo- and Artemis-era materials. The work combined CALPHAD-guided alloy and ceramic phase prediction with high-throughput tribological and microstructural characterization to nominate coating systems that survive the LEO-to-lunar service envelope. As a brief summary of my research:

  • CALPHAD-guided alloy and ceramic design. I screened candidate coating chemistries using Thermo-Calc and Pandat CALPHAD databases to predict phase equilibria, transformation pathways, and sintering behavior across the temperature ranges seen between LEO night-side and lunar daytime surfaces. The CALPHAD pass shortlisted ~40 candidate systems from an initial pool of several hundred, biased toward phases stable under repeated thermal cycling and resistant to atomic-oxygen attack.
  • Nanoindentation and tribological characterization. I built a high-throughput nanoindentation protocol that resolved hardness, elastic modulus, and wear rate across both thin-film and bulk samples in a single sample-mounting pass, cutting characterization wall-clock by roughly 3×. Coupled to pin-on-disc tribometry, the protocol returned per-candidate Archard wear coefficients and adhesion-failure thresholds that fed back into the CALPHAD shortlist.
  • Microstructural analysis of degraded surfaces. I used SEM, TEM, EBSD, XRD, and optical microscopy to characterize the microstructure of as-deposited coatings and to map the evolution of grain-boundary chemistry and phase distribution after accelerated corrosion and abrasive-wear exposures. EBSD identified which grain-boundary populations preferentially nucleated corrosion-driven microcracks, informing a downstream texture-control intervention on the leading candidate.
  • Physical-metallurgy and ceramics grounding. Working from first principles of physical metallurgy and ceramics science - phase equilibria, transformations, sintering kinetics, deformation mechanisms, and environmental degradation - I framed each coating candidate against the failure modes that dominate in extreme service environments rather than against ambient laboratory benchmarks.
  • Mission-relevant performance outcomes. The resulting coating shortlist delivered measurable improvements over Apollo-era baseline materials on the metrics that matter most for spacesuit and structural-spaceship durability: reduced wear rate under regolith-like abrasion, increased corrosion-resistance margin under simulated atomic-oxygen exposure, and stable hardness across thermal-cycle counts representative of an extended lunar surface mission.
