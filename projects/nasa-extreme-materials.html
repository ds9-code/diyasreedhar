Wear- and Corrosion-Resistant Coatings for Spacesuit Materials

Explore Research

Through the NASA L'SPACE program, I led the materials-design track of a mission concept proposing extreme-environment coatings for crewed spacesuit and structural spaceship surfaces - a regime where the Apollo suits' outer fabric was damaged past further use after only three EVAs (NASA TP-2009-214786), and where Artemis-class missions demand far longer surface operations.

  • CALPHAD-guided coating design. Screened candidate alloy and ceramic chemistries with Thermo-Calc and Pandat for phase equilibria, sintering, and stability across the LEO-to-lunar thermal window (~398 K day to ~96 K night). Benchmarked against NASA Glenn's GRX-810 (2× strength at 1,100 °C, ~1,000× creep-life baseline).
  • High-throughput tribology. Built a nanoindentation + pin-on-disc protocol resolving hardness, modulus, Archard wear coefficient, and adhesion-failure threshold in a single sample-mounting pass.
  • Microstructural analysis. SEM, TEM, EBSD, XRD, and optical microscopy on degraded surfaces to map grain-boundary chemistry and identify the populations nucleating corrosion-driven microcracks.
