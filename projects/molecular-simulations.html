Active-Learning Molecular Dynamics for Standard Reference Materials

Explore Research

At the NIST Material Measurement Laboratory (MML), within the Materials Measurement Science Division at the National Institute of Standards and Technology, I investigated molecular-scale simulation methods for industrial materials problems. As a brief summary of my research:

  • I employed ab initio molecular dynamics coupled with active learning on reactive potential-energy surfaces to develop reference data underpinning new Standard Reference Materials (SRMs), used by laboratories worldwide for instrument calibration and quality-control benchmarks.
  • I applied graph-convolution interatomic potentials trained against high-throughput ab initio data to accelerate identification of candidate superalloy compositions within the Materials Genome Initiative, screening configurations against canonical industrial baselines.
  • I contributed to next-generation semiconductor measurement work, using variational methods for Hamiltonian inference and transfer-learned electronic-structure models to construct atomic-scale tools for inspection of chip-relevant material interfaces - the kind of work that supports continued device-feature scaling without yield collapse.

A preprint of this work will be made available shortly.
