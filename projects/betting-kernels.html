Anytime-Valid Two-Sample Testing over Kernel Families

Explore Research

How do you reliably detect when two AI models are behaving differently - without committing to a sample size up front? I built a statistical test that can monitor streams of model outputs continuously and stop the moment evidence is strong enough. The method caught the 88%-vs-49% accuracy gap between Claude Sonnet 4.6 and Haiku 4.5 in just 22 samples, where a naive A/B-test peek would have given the wrong answer. Accepted to the ICML 2026 Workshop on Hypothesis Testing.

  • The construction is the first anytime-valid two-sample test that adapts over a continuous family of reproducing-kernel Hilbert spaces, composing Shekhar–Ramdas online betting within each kernel with method-of-mixtures aggregation across the family.
  • A matching lower bound from a bandpass-Gaussian construction shows the log K aggregation cost is information-theoretically unavoidable; a meta-theorem lifts the construction to kernelized independence testing, KSD goodness-of-fit, and change-point detection.
  • Across six LLM deployment-monitoring scenarios, the mixture beats the post-hoc best-fixed-bandwidth oracle by 1.5× on regime-uncertain streams and preserves Type-I control under continuous monitoring - where a naive peeking z-test inflates to a 34% false-positive rate.
