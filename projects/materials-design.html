Radiation-Induced Degradation Models for Space-Vehicle Composites

Explore Research

Through the U.S. Department of Defense, I conducted materials-design research at the Air Force Research Laboratory (AFRL) on Kirtland Air Force Base in Albuquerque, New Mexico. My focus was extreme-environment structural materials for space-vehicle and directed-energy applications. As a brief summary of my research:

  • I developed first-principles models for radiation-induced degradation of polymer-matrix composites used in deployable space structures, in collaboration with the Deployable Structures Laboratory (DeSel) at Kirtland. I employed variational autoencoders to learn continuous embeddings of candidate polymer architectures, supporting gradient-based inverse design over composition space.
  • I characterized material–laser interaction profiles relevant to the AFRL Directed Energy Directorate, evaluating heat-resistant shield and optical-window candidates under high-power laser and microwave exposure. Graph neural networks were used to control downstream simulations and select probe configurations under physics-based priors.
  • I contributed to validation work for AFRL Materials & Manufacturing Directorate testbeds, calibrating computational predictions for high-temperature alloy and ceramic components. I parameterized force fields via message-passing neural networks trained against ab initio reference data, then validated predictions against experimental measurements from co-located testing facilities.

A preprint of this work will be made available shortly.
