Machine-Learning Quantum Chemistry for PFAS Destruction

Explore Research

Through the U.S. Department of Defense's Strategic Environmental Research and Development Program (SERDP) and ESTCP, I conducted machine-learning quantum-chemistry research on the destruction of per- and polyfluoroalkyl substances (PFAS) - the "forever chemicals" that resist conventional water-treatment chemistry and persist indefinitely in groundwater near DoD installations. My work spanned three SERDP projects and a treatment-train build-out:

  • ML-accelerated quantum chemistry for PFAS degradation pathways (SERDP ER21-1019). I trained machine-learning surrogates on high-fidelity DFT datasets to predict PFAS thermodynamic behavior, unimolecular decomposition routes, and bond-cleavage energies along multi-carbon backbones. Once trained, the surrogate predicts these quantities in a fraction of a second per molecule - collapsing the hours-to-days DFT bottleneck per transition state - and I used it to run high-throughput virtual screening of thousands of theoretical PFAS variants against thermal and chemical destruction conditions, narrowing the candidate set to a tractable shortlist for downstream destruction-reactor work.
  • Closed-loop ML control of destruction reactors (SERDP ER23-5353). In collaboration with Remedy Scientific, I built an active-learning optimization loop that ties real-time physical sensors - reaction chemistry, temperature fluctuations, fluid velocity, background organic-matter load - to autonomous control over destruction-reactor parameters. The ML agent continuously measures reaction state and dynamically adjusts wave frequencies, chemical-additive ratios, and flow rates to hold the reactor at peak destruction efficiency while suppressing the formation of toxic short-chain PFAS byproducts that simpler open-loop control regularly produces.
  • Single-atom photocatalyst inverse design (SERDP ER20-1341). I contributed to the inverse design of a single-atom catalyst (SAC): isolated platinum atoms anchored onto a silicon-carbide (SiC) semiconductor substrate. I ran first-principles DFT on the Pt/SiC interface to study the electron-transfer dynamics at the boundary where the lone Pt atom meets the SiC surface, mapping how the precise atomic spacing of the dispersal shifts the metal's d-band center. The resulting catalyst selectively destabilizes the electronic structure of incoming PFAS molecules and lowers the activation energy for ultraviolet light to snap the carbon-fluorine (C-F) bond - the ultra-stable bond that makes PFAS "forever".
  • Electrochemical-oxidation treatment trains. Because PFAS is typically diluted across millions of gallons of groundwater, I contributed to multi-stage modular "treatment trains" that combine physical concentration with destructive electrochemistry rather than treating the whole water volume. The front stage uses Surface Active Foam Fractionation (SAFF) to bubble air through contaminated water, exploiting the hydrophobic nature of PFAS to trap and concentrate the chemicals on the foam surface; the hyper-concentrated foam then feeds an advanced electrochemical-oxidation (EO) reactor. I optimized the surface chemistry of the boron-doped diamond and mixed-metal-oxide electrodes to generate hydroxyl radicals directly at the electrode boundary layer, slicing through the concentrated PFAS chains at a small fraction of the power load that whole-volume treatment would require.
