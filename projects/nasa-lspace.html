Density Functional Theory for NASA's Lucy Mission

Explore Research

Through the NASA L'SPACE (Lucy Student Pipeline Accelerator and Competency Enabler) program, I conducted Density Functional Theory research in support of NASA's Lucy spacecraft mission to Jupiter's Trojan asteroids. Our team's proposal was selected as the top-ranked submission out of one hundred student teams. As a brief summary of my research:

  • Quantum-mechanical modeling. As Project Lead for Materials and Physical Sciences (Aug 2025 – Dec 2025), I solved the governing equations of electronic structure for Pt-based and earth-abundant transition-metal electrocatalysts - self-consistent Kohn-Sham DFT in VASP, recovering electron-density distributions, band structure, and adsorbate-substrate orbital hybridization on the operating envelope of the Lucy mission's secondary instruments.
  • Computational screening and validation. Working in the materials-informatics regime, I ran ~600 high-throughput ab initio calculations paired with an active-learning acquisition function on the reactive potential-energy surface, narrowing roughly 10,000 candidate compositions to a 25-composition shortlist with a high probability of success in physical synthesis - the same narrow-the-funnel logic that DFT screening provides for materials discovery at industrial scale.
  • ML-accelerated DFT and surrogate modeling. I trained graph-convolution interatomic potentials (CHGNet-style) on the DFT trajectories as a fast neural-network surrogate for the expensive ab initio loop. The surrogate matched DFT energies and forces within tight error bars while running ~104× faster, enabling thousands of virtual experiments in parallel and resolving adsorbate-coverage equilibria that classical force fields cannot reach. I also experimented with ML-enhanced exchange-correlation functionals, training a small neural network to approximate the XC term on curated high-quality DFT datasets - the same recipe that modern AI-for-quantum-chemistry platforms use to sidestep the hours-per-structure cost of traditional DFT.
  • Surface kinetics from first principles. I measured surface binding kinetics through Tafel analysis on the candidate electrocatalysts and polymeric biomaterial coatings, computing Gibbs adsorption energies and overpotentials directly from the DFT trajectories. The combination of true DFT for high-stakes endpoints and the ML surrogate for screening gave us calibrated predictions for reaction rates and catalyst turnover under aerospace operating conditions.
  • Closed-loop autonomous discovery. As Researcher in AI Robotics (May 2025 – Aug 2025), I built an autonomous-experimentation workflow connecting a robotic synthesis platform, DFT, and a deep kinetic model. I trained a reinforcement-learning policy with physics-based priors to choose the next synthesis condition; the resulting closed loop predicts adsorption / desorption rates and catalyst turnover under simulated aerospace operating conditions with R² > 0.9 on held-out wet-lab data.
