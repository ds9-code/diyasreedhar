Density Functional Theory for NASA's Lucy Mission

Explore Research

Through the NASA L'SPACE (Lucy Student Pipeline Accelerator and Competency Enabler) program, I conducted Density Functional Theory research in support of NASA's Lucy spacecraft mission to Jupiter's Trojan asteroids. Our team's proposal was selected as the top-ranked submission out of one hundred student teams. As a brief summary of my research:

  • As Project Lead for Materials and Physical Sciences (Aug 2025 – Dec 2025), I led DFT-based materials-discovery workflows for metal electrocatalysts and polymeric biomaterials, coupling high-throughput ab initio calculations with active learning on reactive potential-energy surfaces to refine AI-generated experimental hypotheses.
  • I characterized anisotropic mechanical properties and surface binding kinetics through Tafel analysis, evaluating polymer combinations for clinical and extreme-environment use including 3D-printed medical implants, biofabricated tissues, and aerospace soft-material systems. Graph-convolution interatomic potentials accelerated molecular dynamics of candidate systems beyond what classical force fields admit.
  • As Researcher in AI Robotics (May 2025 – Aug 2025), I developed autonomous-experimentation workflows for closed-loop robotic materials discovery, integrating DFT, deep learning, Fourier analysis, and multiphysics simulation. I applied reinforcement learning combined with physics-based priors to drive kinetic modeling of catalytic systems and predict reaction rates, adsorption / desorption behavior, and catalyst performance under aerospace operating conditions.

A preprint of this work will be made available shortly.
