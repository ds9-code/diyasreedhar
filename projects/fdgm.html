Natural Ungrokking: Asymmetric Control of Which Rules Survive Pretraining

Explore Research

I asked which capabilities a small 11.5M-parameter autoregressive transformer genuinely internalizes during pretraining and which it only briefly approximates before discarding. Accepted to the Foundations of Deep Generative Models (FoGen) Workshop at ICML 2026 (Seoul). Paper on OpenReview.

  • Transient structural emergence. Across five seeds, I tracked linear-probe accuracy for three structural capabilities during pretraining - end-of-sentence prediction, modal-verb continuation, and adjective-order coherence. All three exhibit the same peak-then-collapse trajectory: the probe rises early, peaks mid-training, and then degrades back toward chance. Lexical and part-of-speech probes, by contrast, remain stable across the same run. This is direct evidence that "capability emergence" curves can hide capability loss at small scale.
  • Probe-specific head repurposing. Using activation patching and targeted head ablations across the model's early layers, I localized the collapse to a small set of attention heads in the first two transformer blocks. The same heads that briefly carry the structural-probe signal are later repurposed for other downstream functions - mid-training the circuit is overwritten in place rather than shrunk. The repurposing is probe-specific: different structural capabilities collapse onto different head subsets.
  • Implication for memorization vs. generalization. The peak-then-collapse pattern is a candidate failure mode for the memorization–generalization story in small autoregressive models: the model briefly induces a structural rule, then overwrites the responsible circuit as training continues. We argue this means snapshot-level "emergence" claims need to be read against the full training trajectory before they support generalization conclusions.
