Biosecurity Evaluation of OpenAI's GPT-Rosalind

Explore Research

At the U.S. Center for AI Standards and Innovation (CAISI) at NIST, I contributed to the unclassified pre-release evaluation of OpenAI's GPT-Rosalind - a frontier biology-reasoning model now distributed to government, academic, and nonprofit partners through the Rosalind Biodefense Program. CAISI led the unclassified side of the dual-use risk assessment: how much biology capability does a biology-tuned frontier model unlock, and where on that capability curve does it cross into national-security-relevant territory.

  • Capability lift over the GPT-5 baseline. I helped run benchmark panels across chemistry, biochemistry, molecular reasoning, gene/disease reasoning, and end-to-end experiment design, measuring GPT-Rosalind's lift over GPT-5, GPT-5.2, and GPT-5.4 on both public benchmarks and CAISI-internal evaluation sets. I implemented the harness that automates the runs and tracks per-task accuracy, calibration, and cost.
  • Dual-use risk surface. I designed task batteries probing the dual-use questions a biology-tuned model raises - wet-lab synthesis-pathway uplift, pathogen-relevant literature navigation, intermediate biosynthesis reasoning - and scored refusals, partial compliances, and safe-completion behavior against red-team prompts from CAISI staff and academic partners.
  • Layered safeguard evaluation. I evaluated the safer-model-behavior layer (refusal calibration on dual-use biology prompts), the bio-specific capability gates, and the access-governance controls that ship with GPT-Rosalind, in coordination with parallel red-teaming at the UK AI Security Institute, Los Alamos National Laboratory, and the Frontier Model Forum.
  • Defensive-use capability mapping. Conversely, I quantified uplift on the public-health-defensive side: epidemiological modelling, biosurveillance triage, non-pharmaceutical-intervention design, and medical-countermeasure literature synthesis - the tasks the Rosalind Biodefense Program is intended to accelerate for federal and academic users.
  • Governance recommendations. The evaluation informs CAISI's voluntary-agreement framework with frontier developers: at what capability tier should biology-capable models warrant access-governance comparable to what sensitive nuclear or chemical-information domains already operate under, and what evaluation artifacts should accompany model releases at that tier.
