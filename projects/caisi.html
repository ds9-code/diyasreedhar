Sparse-Autoencoder Calibration of Brain-MRI Foundation Models

Explore Research

A foundation model trained to predict brain age from MRI scans does it almost entirely through three internal features. Once you find those features, you can correct the model for new patient populations without retraining - cutting brain-age prediction error by 85% on a held-out dataset and transferring to dementia classification, time-to-stroke, and other clinical outcomes. Conducted at the U.S. Center for AI Standards and Innovation (CAISI) at NIST. Under review at ICML 2026; contributed to the 2026 NCWIT Collegiate Award (finalist).

  • A TopK sparse autoencoder over BrainIAC's 768-dimensional CLS embedding, combined with causal masking, isolates three feature directions (#857, #17827, #1796) accounting for ~90% of the model's brain-age signal.
  • Those features biologically validate against canonical cortical aging gradients (temporoparietal default-mode network, occipital visual stream), and longitudinal tracking in OASIS-3 confirms they capture real within-subject volume change rather than a chronological-age shortcut.
  • A closed-form, single-reference Δv calibration on the three features extends to four additional downstream tasks (MCI classification, time-to-stroke, amyloid PET centiloid, APOE allele dose), and the same 1–4 feature axis re-emerges across four independently trained models - ViT, PlainConvUNet, 3D CNN, and Swin Transformer - confirming the signal lives in the data geometry, not any one model's inductive bias.
