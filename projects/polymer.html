Coarse-Grained Polymer Models for Additive Manufacturing

Explore Research

At the NIST Material Measurement Laboratory (MML), within the Materials Science and Engineering Division at the National Institute of Standards and Technology, I investigated structure–property–processing relationships in polymer systems intended for clinical and aerospace applications. As a brief summary of my research in the lab:

  • I ran LAMMPS molecular-dynamics simulations on candidate polyamide and polyetheretherketone (PEEK) chain architectures for 3D-printed medical implants and aerospace soft-material systems, sampling conformer ensembles with replica-exchange MD and fitting anisotropic elastic-tensor components from stress-strain curves. I cross-validated the predicted Young's modulus and Poisson ratio against published DSC and DMA measurements to within ~5%.
  • I built a coarse-grained representation of the polymer chains by training a contrastive autoencoder on atomistic trajectories, reducing each 30-bead segment to a 12-dimensional latent that preserved end-to-end radius of gyration and dihedral populations. With this CG potential I extended accessible simulation timescales by roughly two orders of magnitude, which was enough to capture the slow micro-crack-precursor dynamics during laser-melt cooling that all-atom MD could not reach.
  • I parametrized energy functions for new polymer families against ab initio reference data using force-matching and a small differentiable-MD loop on top of LAMMPS, feeding the resulting force fields into AI-driven materials-discovery workflows associated with the Materials Genome Initiative. The pipeline closes the loop between hypothesis generation, atomistic validation, and the NIST experimental testbed.
